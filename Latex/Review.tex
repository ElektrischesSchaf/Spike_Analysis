\subsection*{Phase-lock definition}
\bookmark[level=1,page=\arabic{page}]{Phase-lock definition}
Activity is phase-lock when its phase is the same or very similar on each trial, 
whereas activity is non-phase-locked when its phase is different on each trial, 
even if it is still time-lock to the trial events. \cite{cohen2014} \\
Phase-lock activity (also sometimes called "evoked") is phase-aligned with the time=0 event and will 
therefore be observed both in time-domain averaging (the ERP) and in frequency-domain averaging. 
Non-phase-locked activity (also sometimes called "induced") is time-locked but not phase-locked to the time=0 event. \cite{cohen2014}
\\

\subsection*{Spatial corrilation}
\bookmark[level=1,page=\arabic{page}]{Spatial corrilation}
Each electrode measures activity from multiple neural populations. 
Similary, many electrodes will record activity from the same brain sources. This introduce spatial autocorrelations. 
(activity at neighboring electrodes is strongly correlated, and the strength of the correlation decreases with distance), 
which limits spatial presision and may cause spurious or inflated results for some kinds of connectivity analysis. \cite{cohen2014}

\subsection*{EEG based motor imagery classification using instantaneous phase difference sequence}
\label{sec:EEG based motor imagery classification using instantaneous phase difference sequence}
\bookmark[level=1, page=\arabic{page}]{kumar2018}
Classify motro imagery task from EEG signal. Extract phase based on phase synchronicity between channels. 
Common spatial patters (CSP), phase locking value (PLV), single trial phase locking value (sPLV), instantaneous phase difference (IPD). 
The most elicited band of phase synchronization between EEG signals is 8-24Hz, containing the Mu (8-12Hz) and Beta (12-24Hz) rhythms which are involved in motor imagery. 
Signal is also bandpass filteded into four non-overlapping frequency bands in the range 8-24Hz with the bandwidth of 4Hz, i.e. 8-12Hz, 12-16Hz, 16-20Hz, 20-24Hz.

\cite{kumar2018}

\subsection*{Machine learning for neural decoding}
\bookmark[level=1,page=\arabic{page}]{Glaser2017}
\label{sec:Machine learning for neural decoding}

Talks about linear and non-learn methods of neural decoding, such as linear regresion, 
Kalman filter...and also ensemble methods, combining different model together.

For example, if neural activity that express a wide array of input/output relationships, 
a method that can express nonlinear functions will outperform a linear method like a Kalman filter. 
In general, a good docoder will incorporate prior knowledge about the outputs, 
but still be nonlinear and relatively agnostic about the overall input/output relationship. 

Many traditional methods assume that neural data relates linearly to the outputs, 
for example, or through a specific nonlinearty chosen beforehand. 
Machine learning, on the other hand, can learn how outputs relate to neural activity 
even if the relationship is quite nonlinear, and thus outperform other methods.\cite{Glaser2017}

\subsection*{Model-Based Spike Detection of Epileptic EEG Data} 
\label{sec:Model-Based Spike Detection of Epileptic EEG Data}
\bookmark[level=1,page=\arabic{page}]{Liu2014}

 This article is working on spike classification in EEG signal.\\

 Using k-point nonlinear energy operator (k-NEO) to dectect all possible spike candidates, 
 and then the proposed  spike model applied only to these candidates for further classification. 
 The proposed models is designed to include both the conventional signal spike model and ones with slow wave-related morpohological features. 
 AdaBoost classifiers were trained and used to classify EEG patterns into three classes: 
 single spike, spike with slow wave and non-spike. 
 The slow wave features are proven effective in improving spike classification.\\

 NEO is good at detecting the sudden occurence of high frequency signals, but sensitive to high noise peaks.
 \cite{Choi2002} proposed the extended version k-NEO:\\
 $\Psi_{k} {x(n)}=x(n)^2-x(n-k)x(n+k)$
\\
x(n) is the input sample
k is a input resolution parameter related to the peak width of detected spike candidates.
The higher the $\Psi_{k}$, the higher possibility the spike candidate is detected.
A Hamming window of length 4k+1 is adopted to eliminate noise after k-NEO processing.\cite{Liu2014}
