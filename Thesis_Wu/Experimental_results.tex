\section{Tap size selection}

We have showed that the attention scores can help to quantify the contributions from each past time bin.
Based on this attribution we designed a method to evalutate the optimal tap sizes in movement decoding tasks for different kinematic.
First step we have to designate a considerable long tap size $T$,
then averaging the attention scores along the predicted sample axis, as showed in Figure~\ref{fig:explain_average_atten_score}.
It will give us a vector of length $T$,
with each element represents the importance of every single past time bin in the whole session.
By plotting the average attention scores with respect to the tap size.
It is easier to find the cutting point for the tap size.


\begin{figure}[H]
    \begin{center}
        \includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{explain_average_atten_score.png}
        \caption{Averaging attention scores}
        \label{fig:explain_average_atten_score}
    \end{center}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Velocity decoder}

We selected the suitable tap size by plotting the average attention score in each tap as well as its first derivative.
By observing when is the second time the first derivative curve approached to zero,
we can tell from which point the past time bins are no longer necessary.
The average attention scores after $t-10$ are nearly zeros,
In Figure~\ref{fig:velocity_decoder_in_different_taps_indy_20161013_03},
the first derivative of the average attention score curve indicated that 11 time bins are all we need to decode velocity.
Spiking neural signals more than 11 time bins ago are not necessary and informative to decoding.
