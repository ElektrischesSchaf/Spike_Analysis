\section{Visualization of attention process}

Here we selected two sessions and plotted the attention map in decoding position, 
velocity and acceleration in Figure~\ref{fig:decoding_trajectories_with_attention_indy_20160418_01} and Figure~\ref{fig:decoding_trajectories_with_attention_indy_20161025_04}.
We have discovered some patterns in our attention maps which is related to the kinematics.
In decoding position,
the focus of attention was shifted gradually when the fingertips stp moving.
It formed an oblique line between each movements,
capturing the fingertips' status.
For velocity and acceleration,
the attention map pattern showed that that decoder tend to focus on earlier spiking data whenever a movement take place.
If the fingertips were at rest,
the decoder will focus on the time bins closer to current time step instead.
It also tells us that the attention module can actually represent the dynamics of temporal features in the point-to-point pursuit task.

\subsection{Position decoder}
% \begin{multicols}{2}
Most sessions benefit from the layer normalization and attention decoder, 
but there are sessions turned out to have particularly poor performances.
Take session indy\_20161027\_03 for example, 
the corresponding attention map is showed in Figure~\ref{fig:indy_20161027_03_corresponding_attention_map},
We can tell that our decoder with attention failed mostly when the fingertips in stationary.
Our attention should able to distinguish if the fingertip of the subjects is moving or at rest.
In these failed predictions,

\section{Tap sizes optimization}

We have showed that the attention score can help as quantify the contributions from each past time bin.
Based on this attribution we designed a measure to evalutate the optimal tap sizes in movement decoding tasks in different kinematics.
First averaging the attention scores along the predicted sample axis,
so that the result can be invariant to the length of each session.
Then plotting the average attention score with respect to the taps.
It is clear to see the influence distributed among all the past time bins.

\subsection{Velocity decoder}

In Figure~\ref{fig:velocity_decoder_in_different_taps_indy_20161014_04} we can see that the average attention score after $t-10$ in session indy\_20161011\_03 is almost zero.
Indicating 10 time bins is all we need to decode velocity.
Spiking neural signal more than 16 time bins ago are not necessary and informative to decoding.
Also examing the attention map in Figure~\ref{fig:velocity_decoder_in_different_taps_r_square_indy_20161014_04},
the attention score for time bins earlier than $t-10$ is almost zero.
